Free-plan operations: Supabase Free is suitable for a small pilot, but projects with low database activity over seven days may pause. One login per week is not a dependable availability plan. 3. Product purpose and boundaries
The platform presents eight realistic cybersecurity decisions, applies a deterministic explainable scoring rubric, gives educational feedback, and permits authorized research administration and analysis. It serves registered participants, anonymous participants, and administrators. It is educational/research software, not a security testing tool, credential collector, live phishing simulator, ML model, or diagnostic prediction service.
Research provenance: Historical Google Form responses and new web submissions are different collection sources. The user's historical count is 93 submitted forms, 91 consented and two declined; verify actual file rows and values during import. Never claim historical respondents used the website. Non-consenting responses are excluded from research analyses and scoring exports. Show source-specific totals and source filters in admin reporting so combined counts cannot be misrepresented.
Scope: Public landing and entry, participant accounts and anonymous session, warning and consent, eight scenarios, result and review, registered result history, separate admin portal, CSV import/export, scenario analytics, localization, security, tests, deployment documentation. No thesis rewrite until the platform is working and verified.

7. Participant journey and functional requirements
7.1 Landing and entry
Public landing displays official logo, product purpose, eight-scenario overview, language switch, Start Assessment, and a concise How It Works. Start presents three clear routes: log in, sign up, and continue anonymously. Account sign-in uses username + password; registration uses required unique username, optional display name, password, and confirmation. Do not require email, phone, university ID, or institutional credentials. Normalize usernames consistently for uniqueness and sign-in, while retaining display casing as desired. Apply reasonable password policy and avoid username enumeration in error messages.
On successful login, registration, or anonymous entry, show the educational/research warning before participant home/consent: no real passwords, OTPs, university credentials, or sensitive information. The modal must be acknowledged. A registered user may see previous results and begin a new assessment from participant home.
Generate a unique internal participant ID in the form CAG-0001 using a concurrency-safe database sequence/counter and a uniqueness constraint; do not generate by counting rows in the browser. Anonymous labels are Anonymous 1, Anonymous 2, etc., stable per anonymous participant and separate from the CAG identifier. Assign IDs according to a documented policy at entry or consent; keep declined/uncompleted records out of completed research metrics. Admin display name precedence: registered display name, else username; anonymous label for anonymous entries.
7.2 Consent and instructions
Present the exact approved bilingual voluntary consent question and explicit Yes/No choices. Record consent decision, consent version, timestamp, and source. A No response ends the assessment respectfully and cannot reach scenarios, score, or research analytics. Do not silently treat page navigation as consent. Before scenario 1, explain the eight situations and answer behavior; repeat the sensitive-data warning.
7.3 Assessment
Display one scenario per screen, Scenario X of 8, teal progress bar, question, and full-width selectable answer cards. Selection uses teal border/light teal background and accessible radio semantics. Next disabled until a choice is selected. Previous preserves prior selections. Avoid revealing correctness, score contribution, attack category, or feedback before final submission. Support refresh/navigation as specified: registered in-progress work may be saved server-side; anonymous in-progress work may survive only the active browser session. Make server-side submission validation authoritative: exactly one approved option for each of the eight scenario IDs and recorded affirmative consent. Prevent duplicate final submissions on double click/retry with an idempotent transaction.
After scenario 8, submit once, show a brief processing state (Analyzing your cybersecurity decisions...), and route to the completed result. Do not simulate success when persistence fails; provide a retry path without duplicate scores.
7.4 Result, history, and review
Show participant display name/anonymous number, CAG ID, final raw score with verified scale, Low/Medium/High Risk, 8/8 completion, a large labelled ring/donut, short interpretation, answer-by-answer educational feedback, Review My Answers, and sensible retake/home actions. The ring must represent a documented quantity (for example normalized score or answered count); never convert an unknown raw score range to a misleading percentage. Use semantic low/medium/high colors only after submission, plus text labels.
Registered users can revisit only their own completed results with dates, scores, and details, including multiple attempts. Anonymous users can view the result only while the current anonymous session is valid; show This anonymous session cannot be restored after you leave. Do not issue a durable recovery token or account lookup for anonymous results. Clarify precise session expiration in the implementation notes; logout/end session revokes anonymous access. Admin access to anonymous records remains possible under its separate role.
8. Scoring service
Use one reusable server-side deterministic scoring service for direct website submissions and accepted CSV imports. The client never supplies a score or risk category. The service takes canonical scenario/option IDs and a versioned rubric, returns per-answer contributions, total score, risk category, and feedback IDs/text. Preserve old prototype weights and decision rules after inspection; the known thresholds are Low >=25, Medium 10–24, High <10. Validate whether scores can be negative, maximum score, and whether any special rules exist. Define explicit boundary tests for 9, 10, 24, 25 and representative eight-answer vectors matched against the Python prototype.
Persist the scoring rubric version and content version on each completed assessment; keep results reproducible if wording or rubric later changes. Re-score imported rows only after exact CSV-option mapping is verified. Unknown options, missing answers, or mismatched source versions are excluded with row-level reasons, not guessed. Imported records must be tagged as Google Form source and must not masquerade as registered website accounts.
9. Admin portal
Navigation
Dashboard, Participants, Assessments, Scenario Analytics, Import / Export, Settings, Logout. Admin login has no public registration. Administrator creation/rotation uses a documented private command or secure provisioning flow; do not seed a published default password.
Dashboard
Top cards: total participants, registered users, anonymous participants, completed assessments, average score, and Low/Medium/High Risk percentages. Each card defines its denominator and source scope. Show Risk Distribution and Registered versus Anonymous donuts, recent assessments, and scenario response summaries. All charts show title, total count, each category count and percentage, legend, accessible text alternative, and empty-state handling. Use modern flat circular charts; no 3D pie charts or decorative unsupported percentages. A source filter distinguishes website and historical Google Form entries; a combined view must be visibly labelled.
Participants and assessments
Search by display name, username, or CAG ID. Filter by registered/anonymous, risk, completed/incomplete, and collection source where relevant. Sort newest/oldest/highest/lowest score; unscored records sort predictably. Show participant, CAG ID, account type, username where applicable, latest score/risk, submitted at, status, and details. Distinguish a participant row from an assessment attempt row; one registered participant can have many attempts. Detail shows all eight selected responses, contributions and feedback if defined, final result, rubric/content versions, timestamps, and source. Never expose password hashes or session secrets.
Scenario analytics
Exactly eight cards with number, total response count, donut, and per-option count and percentage. Detail displays the full exact bilingual content and breakdown, filtered by source if needed. Denominator is the number of eligible, consented, completed assessments with a valid answer for that scenario; explain any exclusion. Count totals and rounded percentages remain consistent with displayed data.
Settings
Implement minimal useful admin settings: administrator's own password change, language preference, and read-only rubric/content version and import history. Do not expose editable scoring thresholds or scenario wording without a governed version-change workflow.
10. Data model and persistence
Use migrations and constraints. Suggested logical entities (adjust naming/normalization in architecture proposal):
Entity	Essential fields / constraints
accounts	ID, unique normalized username, optional display name, password hash, role (participant/admin), timestamps; no public role escalation

admin_audit	Actor, action, timestamp, import/export/settings event, limited metadata


Use true database keys separate from public CAG identifiers. Store timestamps in UTC and display/export with explicit timezone (Asia/Hebron for local presentation when desired). Document deletion/retention and data minimization choices. Encrypt backups and limit data access by role. Do not store plaintext passwords, OTPs, real credentials, or entire CSV uploads indefinitely without need.
11. CSV import and export
Admin-only upload with drag-and-drop and accessible file chooser. Accept documented CSV formats, validate extension plus content, UTF-8/BOM, header mapping, size/row limits, and escaped spreadsheet formulas on export. Preview mapping and counts before commit. Validate consent, all eight answers, allowed option strings/IDs, duplicate source keys, and timestamps. The import summary must report Total Rows, Accepted Rows, Excluded Rows, Imported Assessments and downloadable row-level error reasons. Explain whether Accepted Rows includes previously imported duplicates; use separate duplicate count if useful. Import is idempotent by source file/hash and original row identifier or a stable derived key. A failed batch cannot partially masquerade as success; transaction boundaries and restart behavior must be documented.
For the historical Google Form data, verify actual counts against the stated 93/91/2. Exclude declined rows from scoring and research charts; retain only the minimum audit aggregate/metadata needed to explain exclusions. Import participant records with google_form provenance, not web registration/anonymous sessions. If the form export lacks stable participant identifiers, create source-specific pseudonymous IDs and avoid merging people across sources without evidence. Ask for explicit mapping when bilingual option text is ambiguous. Use the exact same scoring service as live submissions.
Exports: Participants, Assessments, Risk Distribution, Scenario Analytics. Respect filters and include source, consent eligibility, rubric version, denominator, timestamps/timezone, and column definitions where relevant. Avoid unnecessary account identifiers and PII. Escape CSV cells beginning with spreadsheet formula characters. Restrict exports to admins and log them. Never include declined responses in research results export.
12. Security, privacy, accessibility, reliability
- Hash passwords with a maintained adaptive password hashing method; use secure server sessions, session rotation after login, expiration, logout invalidation, HttpOnly/Secure/SameSite cookies, and CSRF protection for cookie-authenticated mutations.
- Validate inputs on the server, parameterize queries, encode output, apply a protective content security policy when feasible, and avoid leaking sensitive errors. Rate-limit login, registration, admin login, and upload endpoints; log security-relevant events without passwords or raw tokens.
- Authorize every result read, dashboard query, import/export, and admin mutation on the server. Test horizontal access between participant accounts and anonymous sessions; protect all admin APIs, not merely pages.
- No direct public access to imported raw files. Secrets via environment variables, with example config containing no real secrets. Migrations, backup/restore plan, and TLS-ready deployment configuration.
- Semantic landmarks, labelled inputs, keyboard-only operation, visible focus, understandable validation, live region for progress/errors, sufficient contrast, approximately 44 px targets, chart text equivalents, and reduced-motion support. Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA in key participant/admin flows.
- Do not claim offline operation unless implemented and tested. Present processing animation as a UI state, not evidence of ML.

14. Acceptance criteria
- Visitor can choose English/Arabic and enter by username login, username-only signup with optional display name, or anonymous session, without email/phone/university ID.
- Warning is acknowledged; explicit No consent stops all assessment/scoring paths. Yes enables eight screens in the fixed order with no premature answer cues.
- Every completed submission yields one server-calculated, persisted result using verified legacy option weights and exact risk boundaries; double-click does not create duplicate attempts.
- Registered user sees only their own historical attempts after a later login. Anonymous user sees full current-session result but cannot recover it after session end; admin can inspect its labelled record.
- English/Arabic content matches approved source verbatim, interface direction works throughout, and original logo and design tokens are faithfully implemented.
- Admin portal is separately authenticated and authorized, with complete dashboard, table, details, eight scenario analytics, source-aware charts, import/export, and honest counts/denominators.
- Importing historical Google Form data recognizes the stated consent split when confirmed by file inspection, excludes non-consenting rows from research analytics, preserves source provenance, and produces the same scores as the direct submission engine for identical eight-answer vectors.
- Fresh deployment passes migrations and smoke tests. Security, accessibility, and automated checks pass with documented commands; no sample data is presented as research findings.
